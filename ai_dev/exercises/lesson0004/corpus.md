Lingua is an app that helps adults learn Spanish, French, and Japanese through daily lessons.

Each lesson teaches a small set of vocabulary, one grammar point, and one short dialogue.

The tutor answers learner questions about the lesson they are currently studying.

The tutor answers only from the course material, never from general knowledge, so wrong facts cannot reach a learner.

Every answer shows which lesson it was drawn from, so a learner can check the source for themselves.

Spaced repetition schedules the review of each word at growing intervals.

The scheduler computes the next review date from the learner's performance on each flashcard.

Lessons are chunked into small units so retrieval can find the exact topic being asked about.

The retrieval step embeds the learner's question and searches the lesson chunks for the closest match.

If no chunk is close enough, the tutor says it does not know rather than guessing.

For compliance, the platform stores no audio recordings of learners.

Accounts are protected with two-factor authentication.

The SRS-2 queue stores the next review date for every card and is exported nightly to the backup cluster.

A daily streak is reset if a learner misses a full day of practice.

Adaptive difficulty raises the lesson level after three correct answers in a row.

Offline mode downloads the current lesson pack so practice continues without a connection.

Pronunciation scoring runs on the device and only the resulting score is uploaded.

The API exposes a sandbox environment with mock learner data for developers.

Customer support hours are Monday to Friday, nine to five, Central European Time.

The Premium tier unlocks unlimited daily lessons and the pronunciation coach.

Learner data falls under the General Data Protection Regulation, and export requests are fulfilled within thirty days.

The mobile apps ship for iOS and Android and sync progress through the same backend.

The Lingua recommendation engine suggests new lessons based on recently missed words.

A learner's consent is recorded separately for marketing email, which is optional.
