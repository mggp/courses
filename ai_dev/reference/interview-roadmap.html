Reference · Interview-first syllabus

Interview roadmap

A short, truthful route from the existing RAG spine to the concepts the interview will actually probe.

Rule zero

Do not optimize for sounding experienced. Optimize for being precise about what you built, what you maintained, what you understand architecturally, and what you have not operated in production. A credible "I have not used Bedrock in production; here is how I would evaluate it" is stronger than invented platform experience.

Current coverage

Strong already. RAG mechanics, chunking, dense/BM25/hybrid retrieval, query rewriting and HyDE, reranking, recall/precision/faithfulness, citations and attribution, context-window dilution, and financial-RAG abstention/guardrail instincts.

Started. Classification versus retrieval: labels, held-out evaluation, and thresholds as policy.

Started. Agent systems and integration boundaries: workflow versus agent, tools/resources/prompts/skills, MCP's host/client/server boundary, and the measurable case for multi-agent systems.

Started. Safety and the control plane: layered guardrails, fail-closed abstention, tool authorization, auditability, and temperature/top-p as sampling controls rather than safety enforcement.

Needs interview-first coverage. Prompt versus context engineering, cloud architecture, and concise Python/FastAPI/SOLID answers.

Truthful baseline

  • Senior Python/web developer; maintained FastAPI services and has about two years of Django development.
  • Built a personal LangGraph tool; do not claim LangChain, AutoGen, or CrewAI experience unless it becomes true.
  • Configured MCP tools and integrated them into a maintained FastAPI service through a library.
  • No hands-on Bedrock, Azure AI/Foundry, or Vertex AI experience to claim yet.

Adaptive interview sections

A · Agent systems and integration boundaries

Diagnostic: strong foundation. Lesson 9 refines the exact boundaries: MCP standardizes access to tools/resources/prompts, not planning or memory; skills are reusable instruction/resource packages; multi-agent needs a measurable reason such as independent parallel work or context isolation. Next step is a short verbal drill after you run the offline trace.

B · Safety and control plane

Primary gap. Lesson 10 covers guardrails as layered controls rather than a prompt trick: authoritative/versioned data, retrieval quality, claim-level evidence, structured output, tool authorization, audit logs, abstention, and human escalation. It also adds sampling controls — especially top-p — and turns the existing financial-RAG instincts into a concise answer. Next step is a short verbal drill after you run the two scenarios.

C · Data and knowledge architecture

Diagnostic: strong vector-versus-graph intuition. Lesson 11 sharpens the precise terms: vector similarity retrieves semantic evidence; graph traversal exposes explicit relationships; vector-graph hybrid retrieval can do both. It adds fixed-size versus semantic chunking and turns the existing RAG metrics work into an interview answer, not a re-teaching of the full RAG spine. Next step is a short verbal drill after you inspect the chunk boundaries.

D · Credible delivery story

Primary practice area. Turn genuine FastAPI/Django/LangGraph/MCP work into concise answers for FastAPI versus Flask and software-design principles. For Bedrock, Azure/Foundry, and Vertex, practice an honest gap answer that continues into architecture, evaluation, IAM/networking, latency, cost, monitoring, and a personal-project learning plan.

After the interview-first block

Return to the prediction track: label design and imbalance, LLM-as-classifier with structured output, tabular classification, anomaly detection, calibration, and regulated-model governance. Lesson 8 remains the foundation; it just stops being the immediate next branch.

Answer shape

For every question, use the same structure: definition → trade-off → one real example → limit or control. It prevents buzzword answers and makes unknown platform experience easy to state honestly.

"I have not used Bedrock hands-on or in production, so I would not claim that.
My relevant work is maintaining FastAPI services, integrating MCP tooling, and
building/evaluating RAG mechanics locally. On Bedrock I would evaluate model
choice, Knowledge Bases where the managed abstraction fits, IAM/network boundaries,
evaluation, latency, cost, and monitoring. Any Bedrock work I build next would be
a personal project, not production experience."

Primary-source map: agent systems, MCP specification, NIST AI RMF, graph databases, and the official cloud documentation recorded in RESOURCES.md.
