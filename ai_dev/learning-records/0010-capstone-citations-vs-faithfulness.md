# Capstone delivered: the full spine in one run, and citations ≠ faithfulness

Lesson 7 wired lessons 1–6 into `exercises/lesson0007/capstone.py` — index → recall (dense+BM25+RRF) → rerank (cross-encoder) → generate (citations) → evaluate (recall@k + faithfulness). The one new stage is generation-with-citations, and the one real insight: **a citation is a pointer, not proof**. The harness runs structural citation integrity (does every `[pN]` point at a retrieved chunk?) plus faithfulness (does any retrieved chunk entail each claim?). The field's stronger umbrella term is *attribution*: citation recall (every claim cited) plus citation precision (the cited chunk itself supports its claim).

**Evidence**: full run on the 24-chunk Lingua corpus, canned answers. mean recall@3 = 1.00 (all six, including the paraphrase row reranking rescued); mean faithfulness = 0.92. Question 6 is the catch: claim 1 ("spaced repetition") supported by p5, claim 2 ("intervals are fixed at one day, three days, one week") marked "not in context" *despite carrying the structurally valid [p5] citation* → 1/2 = 0.50. Retrieval and provenance both look perfect; only NLI faithfulness sees the lie. ALCE (Gao et al., EMNLP 2023) names the sharper check citation precision; Attributed QA (Bohnet et al., 2022) names the umbrella problem attribution.

**Implications**: (1) The retrieval-and-generation spine is complete; the mission's first success criterion ("citations the user can verify") is demonstrably met, but citations must not be overstated as attribution. (2) The capstone's natural upgrade is *per-citation faithfulness*: check each claim against the chunk it cites, not any chunk in context — citation precision in ALCE's vocabulary. (3) A real gotcha, now fixed: the NLI cross-encoder mislabels near-verbatim claims as "neutral" when the premise is the *concatenated* top-3 (too long for its max sequence length) — the fix is per-chunk entailment ("supported by pN"), which is also the semantically correct reading and yields provenance per claim.
