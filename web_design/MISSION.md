# Mission: Web design judgment

## Why
Design personal-project websites whose visual and interaction choices feel intentional. Build enough judgment and vocabulary to direct an AI clearly, diagnose why its output misses, and revise the page without relying on vague taste.

## Success looks like
- Describe a page's intended attention order before choosing colors or components.
- Diagnose a weak interface using specific principles such as hierarchy, proximity, alignment, contrast, rhythm, and consistency.
- Choose familiar interaction patterns when they help, and depart from them for a stated reason.
- Name and describe a visual direction through observable traits rather than style labels alone.
- Give an AI a design brief, inspect the result, and request targeted revisions.
- Produce personal-project websites that remain clear across viewport sizes and meet core accessibility requirements.

## Constraints
- The learner is a senior software developer with strong implementation skills and no formal design training.
- Lessons should be short, practical, and grounded in real web interfaces.
- Exercises should produce fast feedback and build a reusable critique habit.
- The course should connect design choices to concepts familiar from software engineering without pretending the fields are identical.

## Out of scope
- Professional brand identity work, illustration, and logo design.
- Deep user-research practice or product-management process.
- Mastery of specialist design tools before the underlying judgment is useful.
